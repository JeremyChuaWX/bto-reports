HDB's completion and delivery note

Estimated Completion Date (ECD) is the estimated date that the project will be completed. As ECD will depend on the progress of construction works on site, HDB shall not be responsible for any damage or loss arising from a change in the ECD. The ECD of this project is 1Q 2031. Delivery Possession Date (DPD) is the contractual date in the Agreement for Lease by which HDB is required to deliver vacant possession of the booked flat to buyers. DPD is later than the ECD. The estimated DPD for this project is 30 Sep 2032. HDB precincts are planned to serve the evolving needs of the community. There are spaces catered for future inclusion of amenities/ facilities (such as preschools, eldercare facilities, education centres, residents' network centres, day activity centres, etc), commercial facilities (such as shops and eating houses), mechanical and electrical rooms, and such other facilities depending on the prevailing needs, even after residents have moved into the precincts. These spaces could be in the void decks, common property, car parks, or stand-alone community buildings. In addition, housing projects may be located near roads, railways, airbases, construction sites, and land used for non-residential purposes including but not limited to industrial, commercial or utility uses. Hence, residents may experience related disamenities including but not limited to higher noise levels and smell nuisance. 3b. Prime comparisons

  • No resale record yet: no Prime or PLH flat has reached its MOP, so there are no transactions showing how much the Prime rules discount prices Verified.
  • First possible sales: the first PLH launch was in Nov 2021, so its flats could reach the resale market around the mid-2030s Estimate.

3c. What holds resale value back

  • 10-year MOP Official: counted from key collection (ECD 1Q 2031), so the earliest sale is around 2041 Estimate, about 15 years from today.
  • 11% subsidy recovery Official: on a S$1.0M sale, about S$110k goes to HDB Estimate. It is reported to be charged on the higher of the resale price and the valuation Unverified.
  • No renting out the whole flat, ever Official. This removes investor buyers.
  • Resale buyer income ceiling: S$16,000 for families. HDB raised the ceiling for “resale HDB Plus/Prime flat without CPF Housing Grant” from S$14,000 to S$16,000, for HFE letters applied for from 24 Aug 2026 Official, HDB Annex A, 23 Aug 2026. For singles buying a resale 2-room Prime flat, it is S$8,000 (previously S$7,000) Official. This settles the S$14k vs S$16k conflict in favour of S$16k.
  • Other resale rules: at least one buyer must be a Singapore Citizen, and former private-property owners must wait 30 months Unverified.
  • Lots of new supply nearby: 6,000+ homes in Toa Payoh West and 10,000+ including Mount Pleasant over the decade Verified.
  • Working in its favour: the MRT interchange next door, the planned mall and park, a central location, and about 89 years of lease left at MOP Estimate.

3e. Outlook Estimate

  • Discount to resale: a new 4-room here costs about S$300k – S$570k less than the S$1.13M median at the 2018-lease blocks next door. Part of that goes back to HDB through the clawback, and part is held back by the 10-year MOP and the smaller buyer pool.
  • What could push prices up: the mall, park and Integrated Development being completed; Mount Pleasant station opening; general HDB price growth.
  • What could hold prices down: a lot of new Toa Payoh West and Mount Pleasant supply arriving in the 2030s, and no 5-room flats here to lift prices.
  • My view: moderate real gains for the 4-room, but less than an unrestricted flat in the same spot would make.

Construction risks Estimate

  • Sloping site and the MRT next door: LTA’s rail protection rules limit piling and require monitoring near the CCL/TEL structures.
  • Tall blocks: 31 to 43 storeys take longer to build.
  • Long buffer: DPD is 18 months after ECD.
  • Years of nearby construction after you move in: the mall, more Toa Payoh West blocks, Mount Pleasant and the North-South Corridor, the last of which has already slipped to 2029.

6. Summary

Pros

  • About a 3-minute walk to the Caldecott CCL/TEL interchange Verified, with on-site food court and supermarket Official.
  • A Toa Payoh West “second heart” is planned around the station: mall, plaza, 1.2 ha park and car-free corridor Verified.
  • The Integrated Development (sports centre, polyclinic, library and town park) is targeted for 2030 Official.
  • 4-room prices are S$558k – S$832k, against S$820k – S$1.2M for 2018-lease resale flats next door Official / Verified.
  • Close to MacRitchie (about 1.5 km walk); RGS and two girls’ primary schools are within about 1 km Verified.
  • Toa Payoh’s first CCAs, open from age 55 with a cheaper BSP: good for families who want elderly parents nearby Official.
  • Keys around 1Q 2031, a much shorter wait than Mount Pleasant Crest Official.

Cons

  • Prime rules: 10-year MOP, 11% clawback, and no renting out the whole flat, ever Official.
  • Only 2-room Flexi and 4-room flats, with no 3-room or 5-room Official.
  • No dates yet for the mall, park, corridor or school Verified; reserve sites on three sides mean years of construction and views that may be blocked Official.
  • For boys, only one co-ed primary school is within 1 km Verified.
  • CCAs carry fees for the whole lease and cannot be resold Official.
  • Likely competitive ballot for 4-room flats Estimate.

Who it suits

  • First-timer families who plan to stay at least 15 years and work along the CCL, TEL or in the city.
  • Families with daughters, or with parents who might take a CCA or short-lease 2-room Flexi.
  • Singles aged 35+ wanting a central 2-room Flexi.
  • Seniors aged 55+ downsizing.

Not a good fit for: anyone planning to sell within 10 years, wanting rental income from the whole flat, needing 5-room space, or buying mainly for resale profit.

Verdict Estimate: arguably the best-connected project in the Nov 2026 exercise, and priced like other recent Prime launches. It is a strong choice for owner-occupiers, but the 10-year MOP and 11% clawback make it weak as an investment. A 4-room might clear a net gain of roughly −S$30k to +S$460k at MOP in today’s dollars, and there is no Prime resale track record yet. Apply if a 10-year-plus commitment fits your plans.
